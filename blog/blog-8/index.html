ImprovTalk: Building a Mobile App That Teaches Charisma Through Improvisation

By David Gadyan · Published 20 August 2026 · Updated 29 August 2026 · 4 min read · improvtalk, mobile app, ios, communication skills, generative ai, product

Key takeaways

  • Charisma is trainable. It is a set of learnable behaviours — listening, responding, building on what the other person said — not a fixed personality trait.
  • Improv is the right training method because it removes the script. You cannot rehearse a real conversation, so practising unrehearsed response is the closest thing to practising the real skill.
  • The app is built around short repeatable drills, not lessons. Conversational skill is closer to a motor skill than to knowledge, and motor skills need reps.
  • Rapport is the measurable outcome most people actually want — being easy to talk to — and it is downstream of listening, not of talking well.
  • Promotional video content is generated with the Higgsfield API driven by Claude, producing per-segment creative for Instagram, Meta, TikTok and YouTube.

ImprovTalk is an iOS app that trains charisma, communication and rapport-building through improvisation exercises. This post explains the reasoning behind it — why improv, and why drills rather than lessons.

Can charisma really be taught?

Most people treat charisma as a fixed trait: you either have it or you do not.

That framing is wrong, and it is wrong in a useful way. What we call charisma is a cluster of specific, observable behaviours:

  • Listening closely enough to respond to what was actually said
  • Building on someone else’s contribution instead of redirecting to your own
  • Matching the other person’s energy rather than imposing your own
  • Being comfortable with a pause instead of filling it
  • Asking the second question, not just the first

Every one of those is a behaviour. Behaviours are trainable. The reason charisma feels innate is that the people who have it practised it accidentally, early, over thousands of unremarkable conversations.

The app’s premise is that the practice can be deliberate instead of accidental.

Why improvisation is the right training method

There is an obvious alternative: teach people conversation scripts. Openers, follow-ups, stories to have ready.

It does not work, for one structural reason. A real conversation is unscripted. The moment the other person says something you did not anticipate — which is immediately — the script is useless, and you are worse off than before because you are now managing two things: the conversation, and your failed plan for it.

Improvisation trains the opposite capability: take whatever you are given and build on it. That is not a trick, it is the actual underlying skill, and it is the one that transfers.

Improv also has a property that makes it unusually good training: it fails safely. In a drill, saying something that lands badly costs nothing. In a real conversation it costs something real, which is exactly why people avoid the practice that would improve them.

Why drills instead of lessons

Conversational ability is closer to a motor skill than to knowledge.

You do not get better at a motor skill by understanding it more thoroughly. You get better by doing it repeatedly, with feedback, in small doses spread over time. Nobody learned to drive from a book.

So ImprovTalk is built around short, repeatable exercises rather than long lessons. The design goal is a session you can complete in the time it takes to make coffee — because a five-minute exercise done daily beats a forty-minute lesson done once.

What rapport actually means

“Rapport” sounds soft. Operationally it is very concrete: the other person finds it easy to talk to you.

That outcome is almost entirely downstream of listening, not of talking well. The most common failure is not being uninteresting — it is being visibly focused on your own next contribution while the other person is still speaking. People detect that instantly, even when they could not name what they detected.

So a meaningful share of the exercises train receiving rather than performing: hearing the specific thing that was said, and responding to that rather than to the topic in general.

How the promotional content is built

The app’s marketing is itself an AI project, which is a useful demonstration of the same toolchain applied to a different problem.

Video content is generated using the Higgsfield API orchestrated by Claude, producing creative customised per customer segment and per platform — Instagram, Meta, TikTok and YouTube each want different pacing, aspect ratio and hook structure.

Several generation models were evaluated for the work, including Seedance and Kling 2.6 Pro, with results compared side by side before selection.

You can see the video generation project write-up in my portfolio.

What building this taught me

The interesting engineering problem was not the app. It was defining the exercises.

A drill is only useful if it isolates one behaviour, can be completed in a couple of minutes, has no correct answer to memorise, and gets harder in a way the user can feel. Most exercise ideas fail at least one of those. Writing them turned out to be a design problem far more than a technical one — which is the usual shape of a product that is genuinely about people.

Frequently asked questions

What is ImprovTalk?
ImprovTalk is an iOS mobile application that helps people develop charisma, communication and rapport-building skills through improvisation exercises. It uses short, repeatable drills rather than long lessons, on the principle that conversational ability is trained through practice rather than learned through theory.
Can charisma actually be learned?
Yes. Charisma is not a fixed personality trait — it is a cluster of specific, observable behaviours: attentive listening, responding to what was actually said, building on someone else’s contribution rather than redirecting to your own, and matching energy. Each of those is trainable in isolation, which is what the drills target.
Why improvisation rather than conversation scripts?
Because real conversations are unscripted. Scripts train you to deliver prepared lines, which fails the moment the other person says something unexpected — which is always. Improv trains the underlying capability: taking whatever you are given and building on it. That transfers to real conversations; memorised openers do not.
Who is the app for?
People who find conversation effortful rather than automatic — including those preparing for interviews, client-facing work, networking, public speaking, or simply wanting to be easier to talk to. It suits anyone who would rather practise a skill in short sessions than read about it.
What platform does it run on?
ImprovTalk is an iOS application.
How is the app's marketing content produced?
Promotional video is generated using the Higgsfield API orchestrated by Claude, producing content tailored to each customer segment and each social platform — Instagram, Meta, TikTok and YouTube. Several generation models were evaluated, including Seedance and Kling 2.6 Pro.
